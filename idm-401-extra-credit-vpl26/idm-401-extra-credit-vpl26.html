Interview Reflection

1. What responses came naturally?

The question about research results affecting a design decision came most naturally to me. I've encountered this multiple times during junior and senior project so it was still fresh in my head to pull from.

Being the lead researcher, I have experience conducting interviews, usability tests, and synthesizing research findings. This made me feel more comfortable and confident sharing real examples from my projects. I found it easier to explain my thought process when I could connect my answers to something I had actually worked on, especially when discussing how user feedback helped my team reconsider our original design direction.

2. What responses did you struggle with?

I struggled with questions about my strengths and weaknesses because I found it difficult to talk about myself without sounding too generic or rehearsed. I also had trouble answering questions about the techniques I use to ensure accessibility in my designs. While I understand the importance of accessibility, I realized I need to get better at explaining the specific methods I use and connecting them to real examples.

3. What type of feedback did you receive?

The main feedback I received was to improve how I structure my answers using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Although I had relevant experiences to share, my responses sometimes lacked a clear beginning, middle, and end. I also received feedback about improving my storytelling so that my answers would feel more cohesive and emphasize my individual contributions. This helped me realize that having a good example isn't enough if I can't communicate why it matters.

4. Ideas for improving your answers and techniques?

Moving forward, I want to create a collection of stories from my past projects and work experiences that I can reference when preparing for interviews. Rather than memorizing answers word for word, I want to outline each experience using the STAR method so I can remember the key details while still speaking naturally.

I also want to practice answering interview questions more consistently, especially the ones that make me uncomfortable. Recording myself, practicing out loud, and reviewing my responses could help me identify where I ramble or lose focus. Ultimately, my goal is to become more confident in communicating my experiences without feeling like I need to come up with the perfect answer on the spot. I also think it would be helpful to write down my answers first, then practice explaining them out loud.
