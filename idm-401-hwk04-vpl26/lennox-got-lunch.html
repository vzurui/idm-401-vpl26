Overview

Skills: UX Research, UI Design
Tools: Figma
Timeline: 10 weeks

Campus lunch, without the line.

Lennox Got Lunch is a popular Vietnamese food truck serving Drexel’s campus community. Our team designed a mobile ordering experience to make browsing, customizing, and ordering meals faster and more predictable for students and staff.

Working as a collaborative four-person team, we shared responsibilities across research, user flows, wireframing, prototyping, and testing. I contributed to mapping user flows, building interactive screens in Figma, and streamlining the experience for clarity and efficiency.

View the prototype

Challenge

Flexibility without the overwhelm.

Lennox Got Lunch relied on in-person ordering, which often led to long lines, uncertainty around menu availability, and frustration for customers on tight schedules. Its large, customizable menu created an additional challenge: giving users flexibility without making the ordering process feel overwhelming.

We focused on creating an experience that made it easier to browse available items, customize a meal, review an order, and track its status while keeping the overall flow simple and efficient.

Research & Insights

A look and feel as welcoming as the truck.

As a team, we brainstormed features in FigJam and sketched layouts to explore different flows. We prioritized quick ordering, a clear cart preview, and visible order status before developing low-fidelity wireframes in Figma to validate the structure.

We developed a visual direction inspired by Vietnamese food culture and the truck’s casual, welcoming personality. Warm reds and oranges brought energy to the interface while reinforcing associations with appetite and food, and subtle visual cues helped give the experience a stronger Vietnamese identity. Clean typography and simple layouts kept the design modern, approachable, and easy to scan.

Prototype & Testing

Testing what felt intuitive.

We developed an interactive mid-fidelity prototype in Figma and conducted 3+ usability testing sessions across iterative prototype stages. Each round revealed specific areas to improve:

7 of 12 testers

Customization

Struggled with hidden customization options, so we switched to visible, button-based controls for add-ons and meal adjustments.

5 of 12 testers

Checkout

Encountered friction in the ordering flow, so we streamlined the path from item selection through customization and checkout.

11 of 12 testers

Real-time updates

Wanted better visibility into availability and order status, so we made both easier to find.

By the final iteration, users could move from browsing to confirmed order in just a few taps. These refinements reduced friction across the ordering flow and made key information easier to find throughout the experience.

Solution

Ordering that’s quick and predictable.

The final prototype focused on making ordering faster, clearer, and more reliable:

Navigation

A cleaner menu layout with a cart preview so users could track their order as they built it.

Customization

Button-based options replaced drop-downs, making add-ons and protein swaps easy to spot.

Checkout

Payment and order summary were combined into one screen for quicker confirmation.

Profile & Favorites

Users could save meals, view order history, and reorder in just a tap.

Real-Time Updates

Availability labels and order status tracking reduced uncertainty during busy hours.

Together, these changes directly addressed the core pain points identified through research and testing.

Reflection

What collaboration taught me.

This project reinforced how important clear communication and shared design systems are when working collaboratively. Maintaining organized files, regular check-ins, and shared expectations helped our team move efficiently through research, design, and testing.

Testing also reinforced the value of paying attention to small moments of hesitation. Interactions that seemed intuitive during design, particularly customization and checkout, became clear opportunities for improvement once users moved through the prototype.

If I continued the project, I would expand usability testing with a broader range of customers, further evaluate accessibility, and refine microinteractions and real-time feedback throughout the ordering experience.
