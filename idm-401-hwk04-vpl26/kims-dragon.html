Overview

Skills: UX/UI Design, UX Research, Front-End Development
Tools: Figma, FigJam, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, PHP, SQL
Timeline: 10 weeks

Bringing a campus food truck online.

Kim’s Dragon is an on-campus food truck serving Drexel students. Our 5-person team transformed an existing Figma prototype into a fully functional ordering web app, connecting front-end interactions with back-end functionality across a 10-week development cycle.

I served as project manager and supporting data architect, coordinating roadmaps, weekly check-ins, deadlines, team communication, and project documentation while also contributing to user research and usability testing.

Challenge

Making a prototype real without making it complicated.

The challenge was to transform a static prototype into a functional web application without losing the simplicity and usability of the original experience.

The team needed to coordinate design, front-end development, back-end functionality, and testing while building an ordering flow that could support menu browsing, customization, payment, and confirmation. Throughout development, we used user feedback to reduce unnecessary steps and resolve friction in the final build.

Research & Insights

Designing around a busy student’s day.

Before development, we used a user persona and journey map to ground the experience in student needs and identify opportunities to simplify the ordering process.

Our primary persona represented a busy freshman looking for an affordable, convenient meal between classes.

We then mapped the ordering journey to understand where friction occurred across the experience. The journey map surfaced pain points around menu navigation, long wait times, and unclear payment flows.

These insights shaped our priorities around simpler filtering, streamlined checkout, and clearer order confirmation.

Project Management & Development

Leading with structure, trusting the team.

As project manager, I led our 5-person team through the 10-week development cycle, building project roadmaps, setting deadlines, running weekly check-ins, and maintaining communication across design and development. I tracked progress throughout the build, surfaced issues early, and kept the team aligned on priorities through final delivery.

A key part of my role was balancing structure with autonomy. I created a clear workflow and maintained accountability without directing every design or development decision, giving teammates space to own their areas of expertise while stepping in when coordination or support was needed. This helped us adapt to challenges while keeping the project moving on schedule.

Beyond project management, I also supported the technical side of the product. As a supporting data architect, I helped structure product data by building the spreadsheet used to populate the SQL database.

Prototype & Testing

Letting user feedback guide every iteration.

Across multiple iterations, our team conducted usability testing to evaluate how users moved through key ordering tasks. I helped organize sessions, interview participants, and document behaviors and feedback throughout the testing process.

Testing surfaced recurring friction around menu navigation, filters, checkout, and order tracking. Participants:

Menu navigation

Struggled with returning to the menu after adding an item.

Filters

Questioned how filtering options worked.

Order & pickup info

Wanted easier access to order and pickup information.

I synthesized these findings and brought them back to the team, helping translate participant feedback into refinements throughout development. Across three Beta testing sessions:

5/5

Navigation

All three participants rated the app’s navigation 5/5.

5/5

Order tracking

Two of the three participants also rated access to order-tracking details 5/5.

Solution

A faster, simpler way to order.

By the end of the 10-week development cycle, our team had transformed the original Figma prototype into a fully functional ordering web app. Customers could browse the menu, customize items, complete checkout and payment, and review their order confirmation.

Through iterative design, development, and testing, we simplified navigation, clarified key interactions, and reduced friction across the ordering flow. Later testing showed strong usability improvements, particularly around navigation and access to order information.

Several participants also preferred having order confirmation details consolidated on one page, making pickup information quicker to review without navigating between screens.

Together, these refinements reduced unnecessary steps and simplified navigation, creating a more efficient ordering experience for students on tight schedules.

Reflection

What leading a team taught me.

This project strengthened my understanding of how project management directly shapes product delivery. Maintaining clear priorities, communication, and accountability helped our team coordinate design and development while continuing to respond to user feedback.

Testing the coded product also reinforced the value of evaluating an experience throughout development rather than waiting until the end. Watching users hesitate during checkout, navigation, and payment surfaced issues that were difficult to identify from design files alone.

If I continued the project, I would test the app in a live environment, gather behavioral usage data, and evaluate long-term reliability before exploring features such as loyalty programs and personalized recommendations.
